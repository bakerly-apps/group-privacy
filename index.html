1. The short version

You can play the whole game without an account and without telling us anything. The campaign, the builder, Curiosities and the solver all run on your device, offline. If that is how you use Driftile, the only things that ever reach us are today's daily puzzle result and a crash report if the app breaks — neither of which carries your name, your email or any identifier that links one to another.

If you choose to sign in, that changes, and it is worth reading section 3.5. An account exists so your game follows you to another phone, and making that work means keeping a copy of it on our server: your email address, and the progress the app would otherwise hold only on your device — every level you have solved, your best moves, your stars and streaks, your play statistics, the hints and jokers you own, the levels you have built (including the ones you never published), and your settings.

Publishing a level to Community is public. The board and the nickname on your profile are shown to everyone who browses it.

You can delete all of it from inside the app — Settings → Delete account — and uninstalling removes what is on the phone.

We do not sell personal data. We have never sold personal data. We do not show ads and there is no analytics SDK in the app.

3.0 The daily puzzle

The daily puzzle is fetched from our server rather than generated on your phone, so opening it makes a request. That request carries nothing about you: it asks for today's board and nothing else.

When you solve it, your result is sent automatically — you are not asked first. What is sent is:

  • the date of the puzzle
  • the number of moves you used, and the swipes that produced them

We keep the swipes so the server can check that they really do solve that day's board, and so a result can be re-checked later if the checking itself is ever found to be wrong.

If you are signed out, that is all — no name, no account, no device identifier, and nothing that links one day's result to another's.

If you are signed in, the result is recorded against your account for as long as the row stays attributable, currently seven days, so that deleting your account can take it with it. This is a deliberate trade: it was previously tied to a code derived from your network address instead, which made everyone in one household, office or school count as a single player — the second person to solve was folded into the first and never appeared.

For signed-out results, our server derives a short code from your IP address, mixed with a secret and with the date. It exists for one purpose: so that one person counts once in "how many people solved today's puzzle", however many times they play it. Because the date is part of it, the code is different every day and two days of results cannot be connected to each other. The code is stored, never the address it came from, and it is replaced with random data on a daily schedule — the same nightly purge that clears the account link above.

Your IP address is also used, in the same hashed and peppered form, to rate-limit abuse of the server. It is never stored raw and we cannot recover it.

This is the figure shown on the daily screen — how many people solved today — and it is the only reason any of it is collected. There is no leaderboard, no ranking, and no comparison between players.

If you are offline, the daily puzzle cannot be fetched and nothing is sent.

3.2 Android backup

Android may include the app's saved data in the automatic backup it makes to your own Google Drive, under your own Google account. This is a Google operating-system feature, not something we operate. We have no access to those backups and cannot read them.

You control this under Settings → Google → Backup on your device. Turning Android backup off stops it.

3.3 Level share codes

A level you build can be turned into a short code (42 characters for the largest board) that describes the puzzle itself — board size, where the pieces start, where the walls are. It contains nothing about you: not your name, not your device, not when you made it. The code is handed to your device's share sheet or clipboard only when you tap Share code, and where it goes next is your decision. We never see it.

3.4 Google Play itself

When you install or update from Google Play, Google records that installation against your Google account and may collect crash and performance data through the Play Store and Android itself. That is Google's processing under Google's Privacy Policy, not ours, and it happens for every app on the store. We receive only aggregated, anonymous statistics from Google Play Console — installation counts, country totals, crash-rate summaries and similar — which cannot be traced back to an individual by us.

3.5 Signing in and cloud save

You can play everything — the campaign, the daily puzzle, Curiosities, the builder — without an account. Signing in is entirely optional and exists for one reason: so your progress follows you to another device.

If you do sign in, you give us an email address. We send it to Supabase, our server provider, who mails you a sign-in code through Resend, our mail provider. We hold the address so we can recognise you next time, and it is not used for anything else — no newsletters, no marketing, and it is never sold or shared.

You may also sign in with Google, in which case Google tells us the email address on the account you pick, and nothing else.

What cloud save copies to our server. From the moment you are signed in, the app keeps your account in step with your device. What is held there is:

  • every level you have completed, your best move count for each, your stars, how many times you played it, and whether you solved it without an undo or a hint
  • the highest level you have unlocked and the one you last played, including the half-finished position on the board if you left mid-level
  • your daily streak, your longest streak and your daily results
  • your lifetime statistics — total moves, undos, restarts, hints used, levels skipped, dead ends reached and time spent playing
  • which of the in-game awards you have earned
  • how many hints and jokers you own, and how many you have ever earned and spent
  • what you have bought, so a purchase is not lost with the phone
  • your settings: appearance, board style, tile material, sound and haptics
  • every level you have built, including drafts you have never published, and every community level you have played, with your best move count on it

That last one surprises people, so it is stated plainly: an unpublished draft is still copied to your account. It is not visible to anyone else and it is not published by being synced — it is there so that building a level on one phone and finding it on another works at all. Deleting the level deletes it from the account too.

What is never sent. Your nickname is only sent if you publish a level or rate one. Nothing about the device itself — no advertising ID, no phone model, no network operator, no contacts, no location.

Two people, one phone. If somebody else signs in on your device, the game clears the previous player's data from the phone first rather than merging the two accounts together. Their game stays safe on their own account.

Legal basis (EEA/UK): performance of a contract you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) — you cannot have progress that follows you between devices without something to recognise you by.

Deleting it: Settings → Delete account. This removes the account, the email address, the cloud save and everything listed above. It also takes down any levels you published, because after the account is gone there is nothing left to prove they were yours, and it detaches your daily results. The copy on the phone in your hand is deliberately left alone, so deleting the account does not delete the game you are in the middle of.

You can also request deletion without installing the app — see section 11.

3.6 Community levels

Publishing a level sends it to our server so other players can find it. What is sent is the level itself — board size, piece positions, walls — along with the title you gave it and the nickname on your profile, which is shown to everyone who sees the level. Choose a nickname you are comfortable being public; it does not have to be your name, and the game does not ask for your name anywhere.

Rating or reporting a level sends the level's id, your rating or the reason you picked, and a random code generated on your device. That code exists so that one device counts once — it stops the same person rating a level fifty times. It is tied to no account and to nothing else about you, and it is reset if you delete the app's data.

Playing a level from Community also tells the server that it was played, so the play count is right. That request says which level, and nothing about who.

Drafts are not published. A level only becomes visible to other players when you tap Publish. If you are signed in, drafts are still copied to your account as part of cloud save (section 3.5) — that is a private backup, not a listing.

Taking it back down: a published level's ⋯ menu has Take down. Deleting the level from your device takes it down too.

5. Analytics — conditional

Status: NOT ACTIVE — no analytics endpoint is configured and no analytics SDK is installed. This section applies only when an analytics provider is switched on. At launch, the app ships with analytics disabled and no analytics SDK bundled.

If and when analytics is enabled, we would collect gameplay telemetry only, to find out which levels are too hard and where players stop playing.

What would be sent: the name of the event (for example level_completed), and, where relevant: the level identifier, the chapter, your move count, the puzzle's optimal move count, stars earned, how long the level took, how many times you restarted or undid, and — for settings changes — which setting you changed and its new value.

What would never be sent: your name, email, phone number, contacts, location, IP-derived precise location, the contents of any message, or any free text you type.

No identifier is attached — not even a random one. Analytics events carry no installation identifier, no device identifier, no advertising ID, no session identifier and no account. The only things sent alongside an event are the app version number and a timestamp. Two people who solve the same level in the same number of moves produce byte-for-byte identical events.

This is enforced in the code rather than promised in this document. The app maintains a fixed list of the properties an event may carry — level identifier, chapter, move count, optimal move count, stars, elapsed time, restart count, undo count, hint count, product identifier, ad placement, a short error code, and a settings name and value. Anything not on that list is discarded before transmission, and text values must match a short pattern of letters, digits and punctuation, so free text cannot be sent even by accident.

The practical consequence is that we cannot tell one player from another, cannot count how many distinct people play, and cannot link two events to the same person or device. That is deliberate, and section 10 explains what it means for deletion requests.

Your device's IP address is necessarily visible to the receiving server as a property of any internet connection, as it is for every website you visit. Nothing in the data we send is derived from it, and we do not store it against your events or use it to infer your location.

Provider: no analytics provider is used, acting as our processor. Legal basis (EEA/UK): consent, requested through the consent prompt described in section 7.4 before any analytics event is sent. Where consent is refused or withdrawn, no analytics events are sent. Retention: not applicable — no analytics data is collected.

6. Crash and error reporting

Status: ACTIVE. Every release build reports crashes. This is not something you switch on, and at present it is not something you can switch off.

When the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, a report is sent containing: the error and its stack trace, the app version, the Android version, the device model, and a short context object naming the screen or operation that failed.

A crash report carries no identifier we assign to you. The reporter is configured with personally-identifying data collection switched off — which means your IP address is not attached to the report and no user record is created — and with performance tracing and session replay disabled.

Crash reports are used to fix bugs and for nothing else. They are never used for advertising or profiling.

A stack trace can pick up incidental detail — a file path, a device name — that nobody intended to send. We do not attach custom keys, breadcrumbs or user identifiers, which is where that normally comes from.

Provider: Sentry, acting as our processor. Legal basis (EEA/UK): our legitimate interest in keeping the app working (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), or consent where the provider's implementation requires it. Retention: 90 days, Sentry’s default for error events.

7. Advertising — conditional

Status: NOT ACTIVE — no advertising SDK is installed. This section applies only when advertising is switched on. At launch, the app contains no advertising SDK and shows no ads.

7.1 What kind of ads

Only rewarded video, and only when you choose it — you tap a button offering a hint in exchange for watching a video. There are no interstitials, no banners, and no ad you did not ask for. Declining costs you nothing but the hint.

7.2 Who serves them

Google AdMob, and the advertising networks that bid through it. AdMob is operated by Google and its own handling of your data is governed by Google's Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. A list of the ad partners that may receive data is maintained by Google at https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9012903.

7.3 The advertising ID

To show an ad, the AdMob SDK reads your device's Android Advertising ID (AAID) — a resettable identifier that Android provides specifically for advertising. It may also collect your approximate location (derived from your IP address, at roughly city level), your device model and OS version, and information about how you interacted with the ad.

The advertising ID is not your name and is under your control:

  • Reset it: Settings → Google → Ads → Reset advertising ID
  • Delete it entirely: Settings → Google → Ads → Delete advertising ID. Android then returns a string of zeros to every app, and ads become non-personalised.
  • Opt out of personalisation: Settings → Google → Ads → Opt out of Ads Personalisation

When advertising is enabled, the app declares the AD_ID permission, as Google requires of any app that reads this identifier.

7.4 Consent in the EEA, UK and Switzerland

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app shows a consent form the first time it launches, built with Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) and operating under the IAB Transparency & Consent Framework. It asks separately about personalised advertising and about the other purposes your data may be used for.

  • No ad request is made, and no analytics event is sent, before you answer.
  • If you decline personalisation you still get ads, and they still pay out the hint; they are simply chosen without profiling you.
  • You can change your answer at any time from Settings → Privacy options, which reopens the same form.

Outside those regions the app follows the applicable local rules — including, for residents of US states with such laws, the right to opt out of "sale" or "sharing" of personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising. Use the same Settings → Privacy options control, or contact us at support.bakerly.apps@gmail.com.

7.5 What we get

We do not receive your advertising ID. Our own view of advertising is the aggregate revenue and impression reporting Google gives us in the AdMob console.

8. Purchases — conditional

Status: NOT ACTIVE — no billing SDK is installed. This section applies only when in-app purchases are switched on. At launch there is nothing to buy.

Purchases are processed entirely by Google Play Billing. Payment happens inside Google's own interface.

We never see your payment details. We do not receive, store, or have any way to obtain your card number, bank details, billing address, or full name. Google is the merchant of record.

What the app receives from Google is a purchase token confirming that a specific product was bought and is still valid, so it can unlock what you paid for. The app stores the resulting entitlement (for example, "ads removed") on your device so it survives a restart.

Your purchase history, refunds and subscription controls live in your Google Play account: https://play.google.com/store/account. Refunds are handled under Google Play's refund policy.

Restore purchases re-queries Google Play for what your Google account already owns. It does not create any record with us.

Why we cannot delete individual analytics or crash records

Because those records carry no identifier of any kind (section 5), there is nothing in them that points back to you, and nothing we could match a deletion request against. We are not withholding a lookup — the lookup does not exist. If you emailed us asking us to delete your events, we would have no honest way to find them, and we will not ask you for extra personal information in order to try, because collecting identifying data to service a privacy request would make the situation worse rather than better.

What you can actually do, and what it achieves:

  • Withdraw consent via Settings → Privacy options (section 7.4). No analytics event is sent while consent is refused or withdrawn, and anything already queued on your device is discarded rather than sent later.
  • Uninstall the app. Everything on your device goes with it, and nothing further is ever sent.
  • Records already received are deleted by our provider at the end of the retention period stated above, without any action from you.

12. International transfers

We are established in Germany and the data is held in Europe. Our server is a Supabase project in Frankfurt (eu-central-1), and crash reports go to Sentry's European region, also in Germany. Neither involves a transfer out of the EEA.

One exception, and only if you sign in: sign-in codes are delivered by Resend, a US company, which means your email address is processed in the United States for as long as it takes to send that message. That transfer relies on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses together with the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, as set out in Resend's terms.

If sections 5, 7 or 8 are ever switched on, their providers may process data in the United States and other countries on the same basis.

13. Security

On-device data is stored in the app's private storage area, which Android isolates from other apps. All traffic to our providers is encrypted in transit with TLS.

On the server, every table is protected by row-level security: the rules that decide who may read a row are enforced by the database itself rather than by the app asking politely, and they are tested as such on every change. The key that ships inside the app is public by design and grants nothing on its own. Your cloud save is readable only by your own account.

No system is perfectly secure. If you have never signed in, the realistic worst case is that someone with physical access to your unlocked phone learns which puzzle levels you have solved. If you have an account, the data at risk is the list in section 3.5 and the email address you signed in with — no payment details, which are Google's and never ours, and no password, because there is none to steal: signing in is a code sent to your address.
