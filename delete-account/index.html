Deleting your Driftile data

Last updated: 2026-08-20
Applies to: the Android application com.bakerly.driftile on Google Play, and the web version of the same game.
Published by: Omar Diar Bakerly (trading as Bakerly Apps), Herwarthstraße 63, 47137 Duisburg, Germany.

Driftile can be played without an account. If you never signed in, we hold nothing about you at all — everything was on your device, and uninstalling the app removes it.

If you did sign in with an email address, this page is how to have that removed. The full detail of what is held and why is in the privacy policy.

What we hold, if you have an account

What Why
Your email address So we can recognise you, and so your game can follow you to another phone
Your progress Levels solved, best moves, stars, streaks, statistics, awards, and the hints and jokers you own
Your settings Appearance, board style, tile material, sound and haptics
Levels you built Including drafts you never published, so they survive a new phone
Levels you published to Community The board, its title, and the nickname on your profile — these are public
Your daily puzzle results The date and the moves, so the day's solver count is right
Anything you bought So a purchase is not lost with the phone

Deleting it from inside the app

The fastest route, if you still have Driftile installed:

Open Driftile → tap your profile picture → scroll to the bottom → Delete account.

This removes your account and everything held against it, and takes down any levels you published. It happens immediately, you do not have to ask us, and it cannot be undone.

Deleting it if you have already uninstalled

Email support.bakerly.apps@gmail.com from the address you signed in with, and ask us to delete your account.

Sending it from that address is how we know the request is yours. We will not delete an account on the word of somebody who cannot show they own it — that protection is the reason this is an email rather than a form anyone can fill in about anyone.

We confirm within 30 days, and normally within a day or two.

What is removed, and what is not

Removed: your account, your email address, your synced progress and settings, the levels you built, any levels you published to Community, and the link between your account and your daily puzzle results.

Not removed, because it was never linked to you:

  • Crash reports. These carry no identifier we assign to you — no IP address, no user record — so there is nothing in one that could be matched to your account. They age out on their own schedule.
  • Ratings you gave community levels. A rating is tied to a random code generated on your device and to no account, so we cannot find yours. Clearing the app's data resets that code.
  • The solver count for a day. Once a day's total says "4,812 people solved this", removing your row does not un-count you: the total is a number, not a list, and nothing in it names you.

Still on your own device until you uninstall or clear the app's data: your progress, your settings, and any levels you built. Deleting the account deliberately does not reach onto a phone we cannot see — so if you delete your account and keep playing, the game you are in the middle of is still there.

If you never signed in

Today: there is nothing to delete. Nothing about your game reaches us unless you sign in or publish a level, so uninstalling the app removes everything.

In a future version this changes, and it is worth knowing before it does. To let players add friends and play against each other, the game will create an account for you automatically — no email, no sign-up, nothing to agree to. From that version onwards a copy of your progress is kept on our server whether or not you ever sign in.

When that ships, Settings → Delete account works exactly the same way without an email address, and removes the same list above. Uninstalling alone will no longer be enough.

One limitation we would rather state than hide: an account made this way lives on your phone, and there is nothing that proves a different phone is you. If you lose the device we cannot identify the account and cannot delete it on request. Signing in with an email address is what makes it both recoverable and reachable by email.
